> **Quick Answer (Featured Snippet)**: **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)** represents the baseline number of calories your body expends in 24 hours at complete rest, in a thermo-neutral state, to sustain vital functions such as breathing, blood circulation, cellular repair, and internal organ maintenance. The gold-standard clinical formula recommended by sports dietitians is the **Mifflin-St Jeor Equation**: for men, $\text{BMR} = 10 \times \text{weight (kg)} + 6.25 \times \text{height (cm)} - 5 \times \text{age} + 5$; for women, the exact same calculation minus $161$.

![](/images/how-to-calculate-bmr.jpg)

Whether your goal is cutting body fat, building lean muscle mass, or sustaining peak athletic performance, **your BMR forms the physiological foundation of your daily nutrition plan**. Confusing BMR with Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) or starving below your baseline is one of the quickest routes to metabolic slowdown and muscle wasting. Here is the step-by-step breakdown using validated clinical science and real numbers.

## 1. How to Calculate BMR: The Mifflin-St Jeor Equation

While historical formulas such as the 1919 Harris-Benedict equation exist, systematic reviews [1] confirm that the **Mifflin-St Jeor equation** demonstrates the highest clinical accuracy across both non-active adults and resistance lifters, typically predicting resting metabolic rate within $\pm 10\%$.

### Standard Formulas by Gender

| Gender | Clinically Validated Mifflin-St Jeor Equation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Men** | $\text{BMR (kcal)} = 10 \times \text{weight (kg)} + 6.25 \times \text{height (cm)} - 5 \times \text{age (years)} + 5$ |
| **Women** | $\text{BMR (kcal)} = 10 \times \text{weight (kg)} + 6.25 \times \text{height (cm)} - 5 \times \text{age (years)} - 161$ |

**Why the variables matter**:
- **Body Weight**: Every additional kilogram of total mass requires approximately 10 extra calories per day to maintain baseline cellular respiration.
- **Stature / Height**: Taller individuals possess a larger total body surface area, radiating more ambient heat and demanding 6.25 kcal per centimeter.
- **Age Penalty**: Post-adolescence, gradual mitochondrial attenuation and skeletal muscle attrition reduce baseline metabolism by roughly 5 kcal per calendar year.
- **Gender Offset**: Men naturally carry higher median fat-free mass (skeletal muscle) and lower essential adipose tissue, leading to a $+5$ constant for males versus $-161$ for females (a biological resting divergence of roughly 166 kcal).

## 2. Converting BMR to Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

BMR accounts for approximately 60% to 70% of your total caloric burn. To establish an effective calorie deficit or surplus, you must multiply BMR by your **Physical Activity Level (PAL)** to determine your **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**:

$$\text{TDEE} = \text{BMR} \times \text{Activity Multiplier}$$

| Daily Activity Level | Lifestyle & Training Frequency | Multiplier (PAL) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Sedentary** | Desk job, little to no structured exercise, <4,000 steps/day | **1.20** |
| **Lightly Active** | Light cardio or training 1~3 days/week, active commuting (6k~8k steps) | **1.375** |
| **Moderately Active** | Resistance training or moderate exercise 3~5 days/week | **1.55** |
| **Very Active** | Hard resistance training 6~7 days/week or intense physical employment | **1.725** |
| **Extremely Active** | Twice-daily athletic conditioning, competitive endurance athletes | **1.90** |

## 3. Real Example: Step-by-Step Calculation for a 70kg Lifter

To see how the math translates to your plate, let us calculate the targets for a **30-year-old male, 175 cm tall, weighing 70 kg**:

### Step 1: Calculate Baseline BMR
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{BMR} &= 10 \times 70 + 6.25 \times 175 - 5 \times 30 + 5 \\
&= 700 + 1093.75 - 150 + 5 \\
&= 1648.75 \approx 1649 \text{ kcal/day}
\end{aligned}$$
Even in complete vegetative bed rest, his organs demand **1,649 calories** every 24 hours.

### Step 2: Calculate TDEE with Activity
If he trains in the gym 4 times per week, selecting the **Moderately Active multiplier (1.55)**:
$$\text{TDEE} = 1649 \times 1.55 \approx 2556 \text{ kcal/day}$$

### Step 3: Establish Caloric Targets Based on Goals
- **Fat Loss Target (Moderate 400 kcal Deficit)**:
  $$\text{Daily Intake} = 2556 - 400 = 2156 \text{ kcal/day}$$
  *(Notice that 2,156 kcal remains well above his 1,649 BMR floor, preserving thyroid output and lean mass).*
- **Clean Hypertrophy Target (300 kcal Lean Bulk)**:
  $$\text{Daily Intake} = 2556 + 300 = 2856 \text{ kcal/day}$$

*(Note: For a 30-year-old female at 165 cm and 60 kg, baseline BMR yields $10 \times 60 + 6.25 \times 165 - 5 \times 30 - 161 \approx 1320 \text{ kcal/day}$.)*

## 4. Top 3 Common Myths About BMR and Fat Loss

### Myth 1: Eating Below BMR Accelerates Safe Fat Loss
Dropping daily calories below BMR triggers severe **adaptive thermogenesis** [2]. Your body down-regulates triiodothyronine (T3), elevates baseline cortisol, and breaks down muscle tissue to conserve energy. Once normal eating resumes, compromised metabolic capacity leads to immediate rebound weight gain. **Always keep daily calories above BMR.**

### Myth 2: Your BMR Number Is Permanent
BMR changes continuously. As you lose 5 kg of total body mass, the energy required to sustain your body drops. Recalibrate your BMR and TDEE every **4 to 6 weeks** using your updated morning weigh-ins.

### Myth 3: Spicy Foods and Cold Water Significantly Boost BMR
The Thermic Effect of Food (TEF) and minor thermal shifts account for negligible caloric burn. The only evidence-based ways to increase resting metabolic rate are:
1. **Building skeletal muscle mass through progressive overload** (muscle tissue is three times more metabolically active at rest than fat);
2. **Prioritizing deep sleep and high dietary protein** to prevent chronic hormonal downregulation.
