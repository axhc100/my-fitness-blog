> **Quick Answer (Featured Snippet)**: The most accurate method to determine your daily protein requirement is by **body weight (kg)**: sedentary adults need **0.8~1.0g/kg** for general health; resistance-trained lifters in a **hypertrophy phase** need **1.6~2.2g/kg**; and individuals in a caloric deficit **cutting fat** require **1.8~2.4g/kg** to prevent muscle loss. For example, a 70kg lifter aiming to build lean muscle should consume **112~154 grams of protein daily**.

![](/images/daily-protein-intake-guide-en.jpg)

Among all dietary macronutrients, protein holds the crown for physique transformation. It supplies the essential amino acids required for myofibrillar protein synthesis, boasts the highest **Thermic Effect of Food (TEF ~20%~30%)**, and promotes powerful satiety during hunger-inducing diets. However, falling short leads to catabolic muscle loss, while excessive intake crowds out vital carbohydrates and healthy fats. Here is the evidence-based guide to pinpointing your daily requirement.

---

## 1. How Much Protein Do You Need? (Target by Goal)

Broad generic guidelines (such as "eat 50g a day") fail to account for muscle mass or exercise stimulus. Large meta-analyses published in the *British Journal of Sports Medicine* [1] show that protein requirements scale directly with lean mass and training intensity.

### Evidence-Based Intake Tiers

| Goal & Training Status | Recommended Daily Intake (per kg) | 70kg Lifter Target | Primary Physiological Rationale |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Sedentary Maintenance** | **0.8 ~ 1.0 g/kg** | **56 ~ 70 g** | Maintains positive nitrogen balance and cellular tissue turnover |
| **Endurance / Hybrid (HYROX, Running)** | **1.2 ~ 1.6 g/kg** | **84 ~ 112 g** | Accelerates mitochondrial repair and recovers glycogen-depleted fibers |
| **Resistance Training (Hypertrophy)** | **1.6 ~ 2.2 g/kg** | **112 ~ 154 g** | Saturates muscle protein synthesis (MPS) and stimulates mTOR pathways |
| **Aggressive Fat Loss (Calorie Deficit)** | **1.8 ~ 2.4 g/kg** | **126 ~ 168 g** | Counteracts cortisol-induced muscle catabolism while burning fat |

> **Pro-Tip**: For overweight or obese individuals (body fat >25% in men, >32% in women), calculating off total scale weight can yield unnaturally high targets. Base your calculation on **Lean Body Mass (LBM)** or your **Ideal Body Weight** (approx. 1.5~2.0g per kg of target weight).

## 2. Real Example: 70kg Lifter Daily Meal Blueprint

For a **70kg lifter training 4 days a week**, targeting **2.0g/kg** equals:
$$70 \text{ kg} \times 2.0 \text{ g/kg} = 140 \text{ grams of protein/day}$$

Research by Schoenfeld & Aragon [2] suggests distributing daily protein across **3 to 4 meals every 3 to 5 hours** (~35g per meal) to maximize repetitive spikes in muscle protein synthesis:

| Meal Time | Practical Food Combination | Primary Protein Source | Protein Yield |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| **Breakfast (07:30)** | 2 whole eggs + 2 slices whole-wheat toast + 250ml skim milk | Whole egg albumin & dairy casein | **~26 g** |
| **Lunch (12:30)** | 150g grilled chicken breast + 150g jasmine rice + 200g broccoli | Lean poultry animal protein | **~42 g** |
| **Post-Workout (17:30)**| 1 scoop whey protein isolate (30g) + 1 ripe banana | Rapidly absorbed whey leucine | **~25 g** |
| **Dinner (19:30)** | 150g grilled salmon or lean flank steak + 100g firm tofu + quinoa | Omega-3 rich fish / beef & soy isoflavones | **~47 g** |
| **Daily Total** | Complete amino acid profile covering all essential EAAs | **100% Target Met** | **140 grams** |

| **Supplements** | Whey protein powder (1 scoop / 30g) | **24 ~ 25 g** | Peak leucine concentration (>2.7g) for instant post-exercise MPS trigger |

## 4. Top 3 Myths About Protein Intake Debunked

### Myth 1: The Body Can Only Absorb 30g of Protein per Sitting
This confuses **absorption** with **muscle protein synthesis saturation**. The human digestive tract absorbs nearly 100% of consumed amino acids, slowing gastric motility to digest larger meals thoroughly. While 30~40g is the ceiling for stimulating *skeletal muscle repair* in a single window [2], excess amino acids are utilized for gut lining maintenance, peptide hormone synthesis, and immune health.

### Myth 2: High-Protein Diets Damage Healthy Kidneys
In healthy individuals with normal renal function, longitudinal trials testing up to 2.8~3.3g/kg for an entire year observed no negative alterations in glomerular filtration rate (GFR), blood urea nitrogen, or creatinine [3]. Only patients with pre-existing chronic kidney disease (CKD) need medically supervised protein restrictions.

### Myth 3: Plant Protein Cannot Build Muscle Equal to Animal Protein
While individual plant sources (e.g., grains, legumes) often lack one essential amino acid, consuming diverse whole plants throughout the day pools all required amino acids effectively. Soy isolate and pea-rice blends produce virtually identical hypertrophy gains when matched for total protein and leucine content.
